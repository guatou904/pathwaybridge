# Interpreting the evidence

1. Check the input coverage, mapping status and source review flags before considering direction.
2. Select a specific experiment, contrast, time point and biological context. Two different cell types with opposite signs are distinct observations, not automatically a failed replication or a contradiction.
3. Check effect type and units. A fold-change ratio below 1 is lower; a log fold change below 0 is lower. Absolute abundance has no change direction. A model interaction is a difference of differences, not an ordinary group fold change.
4. Follow each reaction link back to the source row. The chemical equation is a reference annotation; input measurements do not demonstrate that the reaction is active, rate-limiting or proceeding in that direction.
5. Keep raw P and adjusted P distinct and inspect correction scope. No significance, confidence score or activity score is assigned by the tool.
6. Keep review-excluded inputs visible for traceability but do not use them as accepted biological evidence. A mapping match does not repair upstream experimental design, normalization or scale problems.

Missing modalities are allowed. Missing input evidence for a reaction is not evidence of no activity. Whole-tissue metabolite abundance cannot be assigned to one cell type because RNA in that cell type happens to map to the same reaction. Cell-type labels do not establish spatial location, and an unknown compartment is not filled from the reference.

Differences across RNA and metabolites can arise from production, consumption, transport and compartmentation. This report does not infer net metabolic flux, causal mechanisms, enzyme activity or independent support by tallying reaction links. Use an appropriate study design and dedicated downstream methods for those questions.
